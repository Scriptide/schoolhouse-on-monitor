The story · 1862 → today

Four lives of one corner

1862 · The ironclad

Before the street had a name, Greenpoint built an impossible ship. At the Continental Iron Works on the waterfront, John Ericsson's USS Monitor — an iron raft with a rotating gun turret, unlike anything afloat — slid into the East River on January 30, 1862. Six weeks later it fought the Merrimack at Hampton Roads: the first battle between ironclads in history. The neighborhood never forgot; it named a street after the ship, and a bronze Monitor monument still stands in the park at the top of that street.

1906 · The school bell

Monsignor Edward McGolrick arrived at St. Cecilia's parish in 1888 and stayed fifty years. From a congregation of dockworkers and factory hands earning about $15 a week, he raised a limestone church, a convent, a rectory — and in 1906, this school: five stories of warm orange brick with gray terra-cotta ornament, a mansard roof, four gables, and an arched entrance on Monitor Street. The Sisters of St. Joseph and the De La Salle Christian Brothers taught here; the classrooms were still lit by gas lamps in the late 1920s. At its peak nearly 1,700 children filled these floors — one of the largest parochial schools in America. Through the Depression, when half of Greenpoint was out of work, the parish fed its neighbors from these buildings.

In 1941 the city renamed the park up the street for Msgr. McGolrick. Your building and your park have the same founder.
2008 · The quiet years

After roughly a century, the last school bell rang in June 2008. But the building never really emptied: the pastor invited artists into the vacant classrooms, and for a few years chalkboard rooms doubled as studios and film sets — at one point hosting some ten shoots a week. The light through those big arched windows, it turns out, was always the point.

2013 → now · The classroom you live in

The parish never sold the ground — a 49-year lease turned the school into 69 tall-ceilinged apartments while the church next door still owns the land beneath your floor. So here you are: writing code by the same windows children learned their letters by, in rooms once lit by gaslight, on a street named for an iron ship, three blocks from a park named for the man who laid your bricks. A century in, the building is still what it always was — a place where people sit in big bright rooms all day and figure things out.

Logistics · the WFH-coder appendix

The boring stuff, solved once

BandwidthHome internet at 11222

  • Verizon Fios covers ~97% of Greenpoint — symmetric 300 Mbps–2 Gbps from ~$35–50/mo. The default: uploads match downloads, which is what video calls and git pushes actually need.
  • Spectrum reaches ~100% of the zip from ~$30/mo, but uploads on many cable plans sit near 35 Mbps.
  • Backup line: T-Mobile 5G Home (~$35–50/mo) or Starlink — worth it if your livelihood is the connection.

Escape timesDoor-to-door, measured

  • Union Square: ~25 min (9 min walk to Graham Av L, ~12 min ride).
  • Grand Central: ~40–45 min (G to Court Sq, cross-platform to the 7).
  • Ferry: India St pier → E 34th St in ~8–10 min on the water ($4.50; a 32-min walk or short bike to the pier).
  • LGA: 15–20 min off-peak by car over the Kosciuszko. JFK: ~30–35 off-peak via the BQE→Van Wyck.
  • Fare notes (2026): subway/bus $3.00, OMNY weekly cap $35.

Packages & hardwareWhen atoms fail

  • UPS Store, 106B Nassau Ave (13 min) for returns; the Franklin St store also accepts FedEx — there's no staffed FedEx office in Greenpoint.
  • Apple Williamsburg (247 Bedford Ave, 26 min walk) for Mac triage.
  • Micro Center Brooklyn (~20 min drive) for PC parts today; B&H via the L for everything else.
  • WR Hardware (720 Manhattan Ave) and Loconsolo Paints (371 Graham) for apartment fixes.

After midnightLate-night lifelines

  • Key Food, 116 Nassau Ave — reported 24 hours.
  • Garden Market deli (162 Nassau) until 2am; Brooklyn Standard until ~10:45pm.
  • Kellogg's Diner — 24/7 since its 2024 revival. Pancakes at 3am is a supported workflow.
  • McCarren track is lit until ~1am for the post-deploy run.

Your peopleThe tech scene, measured

  • Fractal Tech Hub (111 Conselyea St, 10 min) — AI/engineering events near-daily; hosts NYC Code & Coffee Sunday afternoons, one of the city's biggest casual coding meetups.
  • The Recurse Center is one G-train seat away (Nassau Av → Hoyt-Schermerhorn, ~25–30 min door to door).
  • Deskpass hack: one account unlocks Bond Collective ($40), New Work Project ($40), The Yard ($45) and Mindspace ($75) by the day — the whole neighborhood without a membership.

Street levelCar, bike, laundry, meds

  • Citi Bike: 12 docks within 600 m; nearest is Richardson & N Henry — about 150 feet from bed. $239/yr, e-bikes 20¢/min for members.
  • Parking: free curb parking, alternate-side ~twice a week per side.
  • Laundry: Susan Laundromat (178 Driggs, 8 min); Clean Green Cleaners (est. 1982) for same-day dry cleaning.
  • Pharmacy: Saldo Drugs (384 Graham, 7 min, closed Sun); Walgreens on Manhattan Ave for weekends; CityMD urgent care at 795 Manhattan Ave.

Seasons · the annual rhythm

A year from the front stoop

Spring
  • April: Smorgasburg opens (Saturdays, Marsha P. Johnson Park).
  • Late May: Greenpoint Open Studios — 250+ artists fling their doors open, free, all weekend.
  • First blossoms in McGolrick; the café courtyards reopen.
Summer
  • Jun 27–Sep 13: McCarren Pool, free, with 7am lap swim.
  • May–Oct: Under the K Bridge concert season (Four Tet, Jamie xx, Boiler Room have all played it).
  • First Sat of June: Go Green! Brooklyn Festival in McCarren.
Fall
  • Warsaw's densest booking season — 6–8 shows a month, announced 2–4 months out.
  • First Sun of Oct: Pulaski Day — Little Poland celebrates all weekend before the Fifth Ave parade.
  • Late Oct: McGhoulrick Halloween in the park — haunted stroll, tarot, dance party.
Winter
  • Mid-Dec: Greenpointers Holiday Market at the Greenpoint Loft.
  • Pierogi weather: Karczma, Pierozek (Bib Gourmand), and Retro at full power.
  • YMCA lap pool + the lit McCarren track carry the training months.
